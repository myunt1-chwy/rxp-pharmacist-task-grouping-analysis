\subsection{Estimating the Potential Cost Savings of Grouping Similar Tasks}\label{estimating-potential-cost-savings}
In this section, we apply the 3.4\% reduction to a subset of DUR tasks
to arrive at a labor savings in the range \$80,000--\$88,000 per
year. See section \ref{potential-results} for more details.

The labor savings not only depend on the 3.4\% rate of reduction but
also on the DUR tasks that are eligible to benefit from this via
minimum context switching.

In the first following section, we discuss the selection of eligible tasks
in detail.

After that, we discuss the assumptions on scheduling and the logic to
compute the potential labor savings on the eligible population.

We finish with the presentation of the results.

\subsubsection{Determining the Eligible DUR Tasks For Duration Reduction}
In this section we discuss, the historical DUR tasks that were deemed
candidates for the hypothetical duration reduction.

Figures~\ref{fig:dur-partition-sankey} and
\ref{fig:dur-processing-time-sankey} summarize DUR task counts and
imputed processing time. DURs that required a hand-off were excluded
assuming that the pharmacist would lose concentration in this
case. The ``Possible multitasking'' group was excluded because the
pharmacist was working on multiple DURs or tasks at the same time, and
we assume that any context switching benefit would be lost.

DURs with processing times more than 156.6 seconds were
excluded. These DURs have durations above the 95th percentile
threshold and deemed abnormal. The pharmacist is possibly
multitasking.

The \texttt{SAME\_PRECEDING=1} denotes historical DUR tasks that may already
benefit from following a DUR of the same cohort.

The \texttt{SAME\_PRECEDING=0} tasks are subjected to the 3.4\% reduction in
DUR times.

\subsubsection{Reduction Methodology}
In this section, we assume
\begin{itemize}
  \item that the DUR tasks of a pharmacist can be rearranged for a given day.
  \item that stretches of successful DURs are not interrupted by multitasking or a DUR failure.
\end{itemize}

Figure~\ref{fig:same-preceding-counterfactual-flow} shows the
calculation: within each user, process date, and cohort, the first
task keeps its observed duration and later tasks with
\texttt{SAME\_PRECEDING = 0} receive the reduced duration obtained
from the model.
